# R4 — Billing compliance and audit-risk review

Facility-based geriatrics/primary-care practice (1 DO, 1 NP), Tallahassee FL. Research date 2026-11-10. Education, not legal advice. `[n]` = source in the list at the end, opened by me. Quoted words are verbatim.

## Part 1 — Compliance-program baseline

### 1a. OIG General Compliance Program Guidance (Nov 2023) [1]

| Element (§III) | What OIG says a small practice should do (§IV.A, pp. 65–70) |
|---|---|
| 1 Written policies and procedures | Policies, procedures and training on doing the work compliantly; borrowed templates must be tailored. |
| 2 Compliance leadership and oversight | Name a "compliance contact" who, "whenever possible, should not be involved in the billing, coding, or submission of claims" and reports "at least quarterly" to the owner, who "is ultimately responsible". |
| 3 Training and education | Train at hire; "updates and reminders… periodically". |
| 4 Lines of communication and disclosure | Open-door policy, non-retaliation, duty to report, OIG Hotline posted; a two-way channel with any billing company's compliance contact. |
| 5 Enforcing standards | Disciplinary mechanisms in place "before violations… occur". |
| 6 Risk assessment, auditing, monitoring | Assess risks "at least once a year"; "at least an annual audit"; routinely monitor the exclusion list (LEIE), state Medicaid exclusion lists and licences; watch "unusual changes in code utilization". |
| 7 Responding to detected offences | Name who decides whether a violation exists; corrective action plan, "return of overpayments", report or self-disclose. |

### 1b. OIG Nursing Facility guidance (Nov 2024): points for attending physicians and medical directors [2]

- Physician arrangements are a named kickback risk: practitioners "may be in a position to refer… admit patients… certify or recertify patients' need for skilled services"; arrangements must not be "vehicles for paying for referrals" (p. 38).
- Facilities should ensure no "more medical directors than needed", services "actually provided", compensation "at fair market value in an arm's-length transaction", and "contemporaneous documentation… including, for example, time logs" (pp. 38–39).
- Facilities billing Part B designated health services must test financial relationships with "attending physicians and physicians who are… medical directors" against a Stark exception (p. 47).
- A facility "that knowingly refers patients for hospice services who do not qualify" risks false-claims liability (pp. 42–43).

## Part 4 — Fact-check of the CleanClaimRx newsletter

| Claim | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| (a) ABN updated March 2026; transition to May 12, 2026; old form then unenforceable | PARTLY TRUE | The current form reads "CMS-R-131 (Exp. 03/31/2029)" (instructions file dated Mar 11, 2026); CMS allows "60 calendar days from the date of the CMS notification". A Medicare contractor notice (Mar 19, 2026) allows the old form "until May 11, 2026" and requires the new one "starting May 12, 2026". The consequence is overstated: non-use "could result in the notice being invalidated and/or the notifier being held liable", which matters only where a notice is needed to shift liability [50]. CMS's own dated announcement: NOT FOUND. |
| (b) ABNs apply to fee-for-service, not Medicare Advantage | TRUE | "It is not used for items or services provided under the Medicare Advantage (MA) Program" (Manual ch. 30 §50) [50]. |
| (c) No Surprises Act "$149-per-violation penalty" | FALSE | Statute: "not to exceed $10,000 per violation"; adjusted $12,123 (2025 level) [49][29]. An eCFR search of Titles 45 and 42 found no $149 penalty; 149 is the part number of the rules, "149 (surprise billing and transparency)", a likely source of the error (my inference). |
| (d) Providers win 80% of disputes | PARTLY TRUE | CMS: the provider side prevailed in "approximately 85%" of payment determinations in Jul–Dec 2025 (88% in Jan–Jun 2025). That is of determinations, not all disputes (19% were ineligible); emergency care was 52% and radiology 15% [51]. |
| (e) Stark violation "$15,000 per claim" | PARTLY TRUE | $15,000 "for each such service" is the unadjusted statutory figure; the current amount is $31,670 (2025 level), plus refunds [37][29]. |
| (f) The Improving Seniors' Timely Access to Care Act required electronic prior authorization by Jan 1, 2026 | FALSE on source; partly right on substance | Not enacted: H.R. 3514 was ordered reported 42–0 on Jul 15, 2026; S. 1816 remains in committee (official bill status, updated Oct 2026). The requirement is CMS-0057-F: decisions "within 72 hours for expedited… and seven calendar days for standard" requests from Jan 1, 2026; the electronic Prior Authorization API from Jan 1, 2027; drugs excluded [52]. |
| (g) ACCESS model launches July 5, 2026; voluntary; ten years | TRUE | CMS describes a voluntary model: "It runs for 10 years and began July 5, 2026", paying Part B-enrolled organizations "Outcome-Aligned Payments" [53]. |
| (h) Plans under 3.5 stars face significant financial penalties | PARTLY TRUE | No penalty as such. Plans under 3.5 stars keep 50% of savings as rebate, against 65% (3.5 to under 4.5) and 70% (4.5 and above); the quality bonus needs 4 stars; termination risk arises under 3 stars for 3 consecutive years [54]. |

**Is Independent Dispute Resolution relevant?** Barely. It covers commercial coverage for out-of-network emergency care, air ambulance, and out-of-network clinicians at in-network hospitals, hospital outpatient departments, critical access hospitals and surgery centers (45 CFR 149.30). Nursing facilities and assisted living are not on that list. The touchpoints are hospital rounding on a commercial patient whose plan the practice has no contract with, and good-faith estimates for self-pay patients (45 CFR 149.610) [49].
