# R5 — Recognized frameworks and benchmark sets for running a US medical practice as a business

Prepared 2026-11-10 for the "universal practice optimization framework" taxonomy. No patient data.

A source is "opened" only if it was retrieved and read in this session. Confidence: **HIGH** = read on the source's own page or PDF; **MEDIUM** = quoted by a reputable secondary source that was opened; **LOW** = unconfirmed. **NOT FOUND** = searched for, not located. Keys such as [B1] resolve to URLs in Section 7.

**Flagged:** the recognized frameworks treat governance, clinical operations and access, quality, patient experience, human resources and physician well-being, cybersecurity, growth, and malpractice and continuity risk as core; a revenue-cycle-only catalogue omits or thins all eight (domains 1 and 6–12). Only 19% of practice leaders were "very" confident of recovering quickly from a cyberattack (2026 poll) [B17]. Health equity, the fifth aim, belongs under domain 7.

## Part 4 — The physician-owner as CFO of a one-to-three-clinician practice

Read three views every month: profitability, solvency and cash. A practice can collect more than last year and still run short, because owner draws, debt service and aging receivables do not show in profit. MGMA's framing: defend the "spread" between revenue and operating cost per physician against inflation [B11].

| Read monthly | Ratios | Reference point (data year) |
|---|---|---|
| Income statement against budget and prior year | Overhead ratio; operating margin; staff cost share | Family medicine: expenses 60–70% of collections, staffing 20–25%, supplies 5–8% (2009) [C1]; support-staff cost 30–34% of revenue (2025) [B1]. Different denominators. |
| Balance sheet | Current ratio above 1; working capital; days in A/P | [C2][C3][C4] |
| Cash flow and a forward cash forecast | Days cash on hand; debt-service coverage; owner draws | Three to six months of cash or credit (2011) [B48]; coverage above 1.2 (2026) [C5] |
| A/R aging by payer | Days in A/R; adjusted collection rate; denial rate | Under 50 days, 30–40 preferable; at least 95%; under 5% (AAFP, read 2026) [F17] |
| Production, capacity and people | wRVUs and revenue per provider; no-show rate; third-next-available; turnover | AMGA and MGMA medians (2025) [B21][B1] |

**Cadence** (analyst synthesis except where cited):

| Cadence | What to look at |
|---|---|
| Daily | Bank balance and deposits; charges and claims out (MGMA: close encounters within 72 hours, post charges within two days [B16]); time-of-service collections; schedule fill |
| Weekly | Denial and A/R queues by payer; payroll and payables; rolling cash forecast; staffing gaps |
| Monthly | Close the books; one-page "financial vital signs" review, about 10 minutes [C1]; budget variance; balance sheet; A/R aging; payer mix; productivity |
| Quarterly | Reforecast; estimated taxes; payer contract and underpayment review; compensation true-up; external benchmarking; compliance audit sample (OIG Element 6); quality and patient-experience scores |
| Annual | Budget and capital plan; fee schedule; payer renegotiation; malpractice and cyber insurance; compliance risk assessment; tax and retirement planning; valuation and buy-sell refresh; strategic plan |

**Guidance actually read:** AAFP FPM [C1][C2][B34] and AAFP "Managing practice finances" [F17]; AMA [C5][F15]; MGMA Stat [B9][B11][B16]; Medical Economics [C4]; Healio [C3]; HFMA MAP Key definitions [F4]. The AAFP and Medical Economics pieces are structurally sound but their numbers are 5 to 25 years old; HFMA material is hospital-centric.

## Part 5 — Critique of the CleanClaimRx taxonomy

Assessed from the taxonomy as supplied in the brief; the publisher's site was not opened (Section 6).

**Covers well**
- The HFMA revenue-cycle chain: 11 of its 15 KPIs correspond closely to MAP Keys (AR-5, CL-1, FM-1, AR-1 to AR-4, PA-7, AR-6, PB-4, FM-6, PA-4/5, AR-7, and net collection rate, close to FM-2).
- Payer contracting and fee schedules, which MAP Keys ignore.
- Practice lifecycle (launch, acquisition, valuation, closure), where MGMA's BOK has no named domain.
- Billing-law compliance; Stark, Anti-Kickback and HIPAA are GCPG Section II topics.

**Omits**
- Governance and strategy.
- Human resources beyond billing staff and provider pay: recruitment, retention, culture, well-being.
- Clinical operations and access: scheduling, no-shows, patient flow.
- Quality measurement (MIPS, HEDIS, PCMH, equity); no KPI measures quality or cost of care.
- Patient experience, including HFMA's patient financial communications.
- EHR optimization, interoperability and cybersecurity.
- Malpractice, insurance and business continuity; growth and marketing.
- Whole-practice finance: no KPI for overhead, margin, staff cost, liquidity or debt coverage.
- Compliance program infrastructure (the OIG seven elements), exclusion screening and the False Claims Act.
- MAP Keys it leaves out: schedule occupancy (PA-1), pre-registration (PA-2), insurance verification (PA-3), credit balances (AR-9).

**Over-weights**
- Revenue cycle: by title, 13 of 25 operational playbooks (52%), or 17 of 25 (68%) with payer contracting and Medicare Advantage operations, and all 15 KPIs. In the recognized blueprints the same ground is about a quarter: 31 of 135 CPPM questions (23%); not a named domain among MGMA's six in the 4th edition; one of nine review titles in the 5th.
- Compensation (three playbooks) and lifecycle (four) against none for operations, quality, experience or risk.
- Statute-by-statute compliance over a compliance program.
- Format as taxonomy: playbooks, KPIs, specialty guides and an AI toolkit are content types, not domains. Make domain the primary axis, with asset type and specialty as facets.

**Bottom line:** a deep implementation of domains 3 and 4 with pieces of 5, 9 and 13, not a skeleton for the whole framework.

- **Paywalled or gated at MGMA [B4][B5]:** operating cost as % of revenue; multispecialty support staff (2025); specialty compensation and wRVU medians for family medicine, internal medicine and geriatrics; current IT spend.

- **Samples:** AMGA and AMA burnout data come mostly from large groups and health systems; Doximity and Medscape are self-reported averages; MGMA Stat polls are convenience samples of roughly 200–300 leaders.
