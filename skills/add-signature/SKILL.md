# Add my signature

## Record it once
1. William signs his usual signature in black ink on plain white paper and takes a phone photo (flat, good light).
2. He emails it to himself with the subject **my signature** (or drops it in the Drive folder below).
3. Claude crops it, removes the background (transparent PNG, about 600 px wide) and saves it **only** in the
   practice's private Drive credentialing folder as `signature - William Effinger.png`, in a subfolder named
   `signature (private)`. It never goes into this repository (public), a board, an artifact, a digest or a chat
   transcript beyond the moment of use. Deleting that Drive file revokes it.

## Use it
- **Ordinary documents William has read** (cover notes, appeal letters, letters to payers, records-request letters):
  when he says "add my signature" about that document in that conversation, place the PNG on the signature line,
  type the actual date next to it, show him the result, and send or upload only after he says so.
- **Attestations and agreements that say the provider must personally sign** (Medicaid Provider Agreements, CMS-855,
  PECOS, CAQH attestations, collaboration forms, anything "under penalty of perjury"): Claude prepares the PDF and
  William places the signature himself with the PDF viewer's sign tool, one click per signature. That keeps it his
  own act, which these agencies require ("an agent may not sign in lieu of the sole proprietor").
- Every use is logged on the credentialing board row or in the run digest: document, date, where it went.

## Never
- Sign for anyone else. Pam and every other clinician sign their own forms.
- Reuse an earlier "yes" for a new document, or sign on a schedule.
- Lift a signature from an ID card, license or old document.
- Put the signature image in this repository, a public link, or an email to anyone but William.
